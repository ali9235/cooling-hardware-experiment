## Assembly and power documentation sequence

1. Photograph/identify each actual layer and read its part markings before separating or powering it. Do not assume a white layer is a particular thermoelectric element.
2. Identify the purpose of the two fittings and whether a real fluid loop exists. Record any pump, tubing and flow path only if they actually belong to this project.
3. Trace the fan's own supply and polarity. Its printed 12V label is not a supply specification for the whole assembly or a current/power measurement.
4. Document any other electrical element and its independent rating/connection. Do not tie unknown element leads to the fan supply by assumption.
5. If electronics control is present, identify the controller, sensor, switching device, power rails and actual connections before adding software or a circuit diagram. Otherwise retain this as a hardware-only study.
6. Plan a supervised test only after ratings, mounting and any fluid connections are verified. Keep liquids/condensation away from exposed electronics and disconnect power before changing wiring.
7. Record ambient conditions, actual measurement locations, load, voltage/current and time history. Report the observed result even if it does not cool as intended. No temperature or efficiency figure is supplied without such measurements.
